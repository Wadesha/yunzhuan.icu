Common App 主文书

7 prompts · 650 words

Overview Common App Essay 概述

Common App 主文书是申请美国本科的核心文书，650 字硬上限，7 选 1 题目。

The Common App Essay is the central personal essay required by most US undergraduate applications through the Common Application. Applicants choose one of seven prompts and write a single essay of up to 650 words (a hard limit enforced by the system). The essay goes to every Common App school on your list, so it should be broadly personal rather than school-specific. The 2025-26 prompts are largely stable, with the option to write on a topic of your choice.

Prompts Analysis 7 个题目分析

2025-26 年 7 个题目框架，核心都是让你讲一个关于"你是谁"的故事。

  • P1Background, Identity, Interest, Talent
    "Some students have a background, identity, interest, or talent that is so meaningful they believe their application would be incomplete without it." Best for a defining personal trait or community.
  • P2Lessons from Obstacles / Failure
    "The lessons we take from obstacles we encounter can be fundamental to later success." Best for a setback that drove growth; focus on reflection, not the failure itself.
  • P3Challenging a Belief or Idea
    "Reflect on a time when you questioned or challenged a belief or idea." Best for intellectual independence; show the process of questioning, not just the conclusion.
  • P4Gratitude / Unexpected Kindness
    "Reflect on something that someone has done for you that has made you happy or thankful in a surprising way." Best for showing reciprocity and emotional maturity.
  • P5Accomplishment that Sparked Growth
    "Discuss an accomplishment, event, or realization that sparked a period of personal growth." Best for a turning point; show change in self-understanding.
  • P6Captivating Topic / Idea
    "Describe a topic, idea, or concept you find so engaging that it makes you lose all track of time." Best for intellectual curiosity; show why it matters to you.
  • P7Topic of Your Choice
    "Share an essay on any topic of your choice." Use only if no other prompt fits; the essay must still reveal who you are.

Topic Selection 选题策略

先选故事，再匹配题目；故事本身比题目编号更重要。

  • Story first, prompt second (先故事后题目)
    Draft a list of 5-10 meaningful experiences, then see which prompt each naturally fits. Do not pick a prompt and force a story.
  • Depth over breadth (深度优先)
    One well-told story with reflection beats several loosely connected anecdotes.
  • Avoid resume overlap (避免与简历重复)
    The essay should add a dimension not visible in grades, test scores and activity lists.
  • Test the theme (检验主题)
    Ask: could a friend write this essay? If yes, it is too generic. The story must be uniquely yours.

Structure & Narrative 结构与叙事

叙事弧线：场景引入 → 冲突发展 → 转折反思 → 主题升华。

Hook (开头钩子)Open in a specific moment; avoid broad generalisations about life or society
Context (背景)Provide just enough setting for the reader to understand the scene
Action / Conflict (行动与冲突)Show what you did, decided or struggled with; tension drives narrative
Reflection (反思)Explain what you learned and how you changed; this is the essay's core
Conclusion (结尾)Connect back to the opening; project forward without over-promising
Word budget (字数分配)Roughly 100 opening + 400 body + 150 closing, adjusted to the story

Revision Checklist 修改清单

定稿前逐项核对，重点检查主题、细节、字数与语气。

  • Theme clarity (主题清晰)
    Can a reader state your main message in one sentence after reading?
  • Specific evidence (具体证据)
    Every claim about your character is backed by a concrete scene or action.
  • Word count (字数)
    Between 250 and 650 words; the system cuts off anything beyond 650.
  • Prompt fit (切题)
    The essay genuinely answers the chosen prompt, not just loosely relates to it.
  • Voice (语气)
    Sounds like a 17- or 18-year-old, not a thesaurus or a parent.
  • Opening and closing (首尾呼应)
    The ending reframes or completes the opening; no new topics introduced at the close.
  • Grammar and flow (语法与流畅)
    Read aloud; fix awkward phrasing, run-on sentences and passive voice.

Examples Framework 范文框架

此处只列框架结构，不提供完整范文；可参照官方成功文书示例。

Example A (示例 A)Theme: intellectual curiosity (P6). Structure: a childhood question → self-driven research → a moment of insight → how it shapes future study.
Example B (示例 B)Theme: identity and community (P1). Structure: a family ritual → tension between two cultures → reconciliation → what it teaches about perspective.
Example C (示例 C)Theme: growth from setback (P2). Structure: a public failure → initial reaction → reframing → concrete change in approach.
Example D (示例 D)Theme: challenging a belief (P3). Structure: an inherited assumption → an encounter that questions it → uncomfortable questioning → revised view.
What to learn (学习要点)Note the specificity of detail, the proportion of reflection vs narrative, and the authentic teenage voice in each.
